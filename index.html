Tracing Australia’s Greenhouse Gas Story 🌿

Australia’s greenhouse story is one of ambition and transformation.
From bustling cities to vast forests, every region plays a part.
Between 2017 and 2022, emissions rose, fell, and reset priorities.
COVID lockdowns caused a dramatic dip — a pause in impact.
Recovery brought new challenges and a sharper focus on net zero.

Queensland and New South Wales dominate national emissions overall,
while Tasmania stands out — quietly absorbing more than it emits.
Scroll to uncover how each state’s story unfolds across years,

🏃‍♂️💨 Who’s the Most Efficient?

Each tile captures how much carbon each person is responsible for — a snapshot of efficiency, not size. Darker shades signal heavier footprints; lighter ones, a shift toward cleaner living.

Notice the trend: states like Victoria and New South Wales gradually lighten, reflecting cleaner grids and lifestyle changes. Queensland and Western Australia stay darker — energy-intensive industries and mining keep their per-capita emissions high.

Why this view? Looking at emissions per person reveals who’s leading in efficiency. Smaller regions such as the ACT look impressively light, powered almost entirely by renewables, while resource-heavy states show how geography and economy shape climate outcomes.

🥧 ➡️ 📊 How does Australia’s emission mix evolve?

Australia’s greenhouse gas composition has gradually shifted in recent years, reflecting both technological progress and changing land-use patterns. The stacked bar chart illustrates how total emissions are distributed across sectors from 2017 to 2022, revealing a steady decline in Energy-related emissions — once the nation’s dominant source.

Meanwhile, Land Use, Land-Use Change and Forestry (LULUCF) plays a growing role in offsetting national totals, with negative values representing carbon absorbed by forests and restored ecosystems. Together with a modest reduction in Industrial Processes and Waste, these shifts suggest that Australia’s transition toward cleaner energy and improved carbon management is gradually taking effect.

Why this view? Showing both sector proportions and totals over time helps explain not just how much emissions change, but why — highlighting the interaction between industrial decline, renewable adoption, and land-based carbon sinks.

Key insights:
✅ Geography matters: big states emit more.
✅ 2020 dip shows COVID’s impact on emissions.
✅ Energy drives output; land use offsets carbon.
✅ Efficiency improves, unevenly across states.
