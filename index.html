Australia • Greenhouse Gas Emissions • 2017–2022

Tracing Australia’s Greenhouse Gas Story

Australia’s greenhouse gas story isn’t flat — it’s a map of energy, land, and people constantly shifting. This dashboard unpacks the journey from 2017 to 2022 — six years that saw steady progress, a global pandemic, and new ambitions for net zero.

Queensland and New South Wales glow darkest across the years, carrying much of the emissions weight, while Tasmania stays pale — its forests act like lungs, pulling carbon back in. Scroll down and explore how each state tells its own story — where emissions happen, how they’ve changed, and what drives the rise or fall.

Where are emissions concentrated?

The choropleth map paints the big picture — darker shades mean higher total emissions. QLD and NSW dominate the map, while TAS stands out as carbon-negative. Slide through the years to see how patterns shift, with a visible dip in 2020 during the pandemic.

How are the states changing?

Each small panel tells a mini-story of its state. Most trend slightly downward, signalling gradual progress. Around 2020, a sharp drop appears almost everywhere — the impact of COVID-19 — followed by partial rebounds as industries restarted.

What drives the rise or fall?

The stacked area chart breaks down each state’s total by sector. Energy is the biggest slice across Australia, but negative values in Land-Use, Land-Use Change & Forestry (LULUCF) reveal nature’s role in offsetting emissions — especially in Tasmania, where it flips the total below zero.

Who’s the most efficient?

The heatmap compares emission intensity per person (t CO₂-e per capita). Darker tiles mean heavier footprints; lighter ones mark improvement. Efficiency gains are visible in some states — proof that cleaner energy and behavioural shifts are taking hold.

Australia’s sector mix by year

The donut chart shows the share of each sector’s contribution for the selected year. Hover to reveal exact values — notice how Energy still dominates but LULUCF increasingly offsets total emissions.

How sectors evolve over time

The stacked bar chart highlights how each sector’s signed contribution shifts year to year. Together, these bars reveal a story of rebalancing — slower emissions growth, stronger offsets, and ongoing energy transition.

Key insights:
• Geography shapes emissions — larger, energy-intensive states lead the charts.
• 2020 marked a nationwide dip, showing how behaviour and industry shutdowns affect totals.
• Energy and land use are the main levers — the first drives emissions, the second offsets them.
• Efficiency improvements are visible but uneven across states.
